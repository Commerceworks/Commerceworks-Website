The gap

The lorry left. The invoice didn't.

A load is lifted at a terminal. Somewhere between the gantry and the general ledger, that movement has to become a bill of lading, a certificate of analysis, a freight cost, a sales order in SAP, a line on a reconciliation and eventually an invoice at the right price under the right incoterm.

Most businesses in this sector do that with a core ERP, a scheduling system, a terminal automation system, three spreadsheets and somebody who knows where the bodies are buried. It works, until the person leaves or the volume doubles.

We close those gaps. Not by replacing the ERP, which nobody wants, but by building the connectors between what you already run, and the operational apps that sit in the spaces the ERP was never going to cover.

How the work runs

Our longest engagement began as a scoping call about tanks and products. It is still running.

The pattern repeats. We are brought in to support an estate somebody else built. We stabilise it, then start closing the gaps around it: order and commercial data flows, reporting built on transactions rather than extracts, and eventually systems built from scratch where nothing suitable existed.

These are not projects. They are working relationships, and the longest of them has outlasted several of the systems it connects.

  • Phase one

    Support and stabilisation. Case work on an estate we did not build: orders, contracts, users, SAP exports and the file drops that keep failing at three in the morning.

  • Phase two

    Order to invoice. Freight quoting, invoice point logic, COA handling, tank and stock configuration, inventory reporting and export documentation.

  • Phase three

    Reporting. Power BI for rail and fleet utilisation, procurement, inventory and hedging, modelled on transaction data rather than a monthly extract.

  • Phase four

    Platform build. Where the ERP was never going to reach: a Power Platform system for freight cost, purchasing and reconciliation. Thousands of hours in, still being extended.
